Linguistics & computation

Gordon Tin

I use mathematical and computational tools to study how phonological rules interact. I’m especially interested in opacity and in what formal models retain about individual rule applications.

About

I graduated from UC San Diego with a BA in Linguistics and a BS in Mathematics–Computer Science. My current research asks how to identify individual phonological rule applications when their contexts overlap, and what a formal model needs to record about the relationship between input and output. I’ve also worked on finite-state morphological parsing for Tira and on the computational complexity of a flower placement problem.

Research interests

I’m interested in phonological opacity, where the effects of a rule aren’t obvious from the surface form. I’m interested in using finite-state transducers to model how one string changes into another, and in what these models can tell us about which parts of the input correspond to which parts of the output. More broadly, I’d like to see how algebra can make phonological models more precise. Outside linguistics, I enjoy graph theory, combinatorics, and complexity questions.

Counting Overlapping Rule Applications: An Interval-Cover Approach to Nonvacuous Breaks

An interval-cover framework for counting phonological rule applications when they share part of their licensing context. The manuscript compares this extension with ordinary nonvacuous breaks.

Computational complexity

NP-Completeness of One-Round Flower Placement on King Grids

A reduction from planar monotone 2-in-4 SAT establishes NP-completeness for the threshold decision problem on king grids with blocked cells, and NP-hardness for the corresponding optimization problem.

Acknowledgments: Thanks to Daniel Kane (UC San Diego, Mathematics and Computer Science & Engineering) for guidance and feedback on the exposition; to Gency Dela Torre (UC Riverside alumnus) for introducing me to the problem and explaining the flower-breeding mechanics; and to Patrick Liu (UC Riverside) for walking me through the code he and Gency developed to optimize smaller grids.

Research software

Tira morphological parser

I contributed to Mark Simmons’s UC San Diego project, which uses Pynini to build finite-state transducers for Tira morphology. I checked parser analyses against Tira data and glosses, and fixed bugs in how the parser builds and applies lexical-class conditions for prefix and suffix rules. I also built a Streamlit dashboard to inspect the configuration files the parser had loaded.

Language Loom

I wrote the original proof of concept as a Discord bot to look up unfamiliar Chinese words and prepare material for Anki flashcards. I now collaborate with two friends on the app, contributing linguistic guidance, finding resources such as corpora, and reviewing new features.
